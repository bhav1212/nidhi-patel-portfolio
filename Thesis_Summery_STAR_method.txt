1. Business problem

Is cash-access infrastructure (ATMs, bank branches, and cashback points) appropriately distributed across Germany? Raw counts can be misleading: a rural region with few ATMs may be adequately served, while a city with many may not. I reframed this into two questions: Which regions are unusual compared to socioeconomically similar regions, and which factors most influence cash-access resource distribution? This is a contextual anomaly detection problem with a focus on interpretability.

2. The Challenge

- A complete, centralized view of cash access point locations across Germany did not exist
- No ground-truth data was available to determine whether a region was over-served or under-served.
- The analysis and resulting recommendations needed to be interpretable and easily explainable to business stakeholders.

3. My approach

- Scraped 75,000+ locations, which were spread across 10+ banks locator websites. and cleaned the data into one verified source-of-truth database.
- Defined “context” using eight socioeconomic groups, such as (Household/Sectorial) economics, unemployment, taxation, workforce and demographics, etc.
- Researched and implemented different contextual anomaly detection methods/frameworks considering the interpretability as most important by product in mind
- Compared all frameworks on German data and 16 benchmark datasets.
- Explained the results along with their responsible underlying factors with SHAP and feature importance.

4. The result

- Built the first consolidated database of Germany’s cash-access network.
- Raw counts favour big cities, but context-adjusted views revealed well-served rural regions and possible under-provision in parts of Thüringen, Brandenburg and Rheinland-Pfalz.
- This gave the business an explainable way to prioritise which regions to investigate.

5. What I learned
I can approach unfamiliar topics independently, break complex problems into smaller tasks, and learn whatever is needed to reach the goal. I also learned that a trustworthy dataset matters as much as the model, and that reframing the question (“unusual compared to what?”) is half the solution.
